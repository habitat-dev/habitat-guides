Housekeeping

Here's the real talk on trip info you need right away. We want you on the right trip and having the best week possible.

  • This is a retreat, not a workshop. There are no styled shoots, content days, etc. Obviously bring your camera if you want it! But no one is forcing you to if you don't want to. Also, everything is optional. If you would rather skip the Hot Air Balloon ride and spend the afternoon by the pool, go for it!
  • Leave the drone at home. Drones are banned in Morocco. Customs confiscates them at the airport and you won't get it back. Cameras are completely fine.
  • The medina is a maze. Cars can't get into most of the old city, so the last stretch to the riad is on foot, with a porter and a cart for your bags. Pack a bag you can live with being wheeled down dirt or cobbled streets.
  • Marrakech is a lot, in the best way. Motorbikes in the alleys, shopkeepers calling you in, the call to prayer five times a day. It's loud and it's alive. Then you walk through the riad door and it's quiet. Day 4, the desert is the reset.
  • Dress code. Morocco is a Muslim country and we want to respect local customs. Please bring an outfit that covers your knees and shoulders, especially for the market day. This can be a shawl you throw on or a long skirt. We just ask that you be prepared and respectful.
  • Late March is warm by day, cold at night. Around 22°C / 72°F in the afternoon. The desert drops much colder after sunset. Bring layers.
  • Wi-Fi. Yes, there is wifi available both at the Riad and at the Desert Camp.
  • You're sharing space with people you may not know. Most people arrive solo, some come with friends. If something is bothering you, tell us early (see the Anonymous Feedback Form in section 10). We can fix things in real time. Don't wait until the week is over to put it out there. That's a disservice to you and to the people around you.
  • Plans can change. Weather will determine our Hot Air Balloon ride, which may require us to rearrange the itinerary. We try our best to stick to the plan, but occasionally we have to adapt. We will always communicate if that needs to happen, and just ask that you remain flexible with the situation while we sort through it.

Getting there + getting home

You book your own flights, so you can come from anywhere and add travel on either side. Booking your own doesn't mean you're on your own. Send us any flight before you book if you want our eyes on it.

The only two times you need

Arrive

Land at Marrakesh Menara Airport (RAK) whenever works for you on Friday, March 26. Our first group get-together is at 5pm.

Depart

Book the latest flight possible on Wednesday, March 31. We have to drive about an hour and a half from the desert to get back to the airport.

How you get there is up to you. Many people add a few days on the front or back end. Want help planning that part? Tell us.

Transfers: All handled by BE Agafay. Everyone has a personal car, from RAK to the riad on arrival day, from the riad to the desert on Day 4, and from the desert back to RAK on departure day. If a few of you land around the same time we'll group you together, but there's no transfer window. The airport is only about 4 km / 2.5 miles from the riad.

Arriving early or staying on? No problem. Let us know and we'll help you arrange your own transportation.

Route options

Most people connect through Europe (Paris, Madrid, Lisbon, London) or fly overnight from New York to Casablanca and connect to Marrakech. Plenty of carriers fly into Marrakech from Europe.

Our flight advice

  • Look at flights first. Find prices and times that work for you.
  • Book in the sweet spot. For Morocco that's usually 3 to 6 months out, so October to December for this trip. Booking much earlier rarely saves money, and fares tend to climb in the last couple of months.
  • Check them against the key times above. Send your flights to us before you book if you're unsure.
  • Once booked, fill out the Flight + Info Form. It also collects dietary needs, health notes and emergency contact. This is how we plan your car.

Running late or stuck?

Email us at habitat@habitatretreats.com as soon as you know. On travel day you can also text or WhatsApp Carrigan (+1 903 515 0061). We'll rework your car. If you can't reach us, call Riad BE Marrakech at +212 524 38 45 11.

Passports, health + safety

Passport + entry

  • Your passport must be valid until at least September 30, 2027, with at least 1 blank page. Check it today.
  • US citizens: no visa needed for stays under 90 days.
  • Entry is simple: show your passport at the airport and it gets stamped. Make sure it actually gets stamped, or leaving can be a hassle.
  • No drones. They may be confiscated at the airport.
  • Renewing your passport before the trip? Send us the new details.
  • Save a photo of your passport to your phone, just in case.

Health

  • Vaccines: none required. The CDC recommends being up to date on routine vaccines, plus hepatitis A and typhoid for most travelers. Check the CDC travel page for Morocco and talk to your doctor 4 to 6 weeks before.
  • Tell us about allergies, injuries, pregnancy, or health conditions in your Flight + Info Form. It stays between us and the people who need to know (the kitchen, your leaders). Please be thorough. The more we know, the better we can look after you.
  • Bring any prescriptions in original packaging with a copy of the prescription, plus a few extra days' supply just in case.
  • Pharmacies (look for the green cross) are everywhere in Marrakech.
  • Drink bottled water. Skip ice and raw salads from street stalls for the first few days.
  • The Moroccan sun is strong, even in March. SPF, hat and water.

Safety

Marrakech is a busy, welcoming city and very used to visitors. Normal travel sense applies.

  • Keep your passport in the room safe.
  • Pickpockets work the souks and Jemaa el-Fnaa. Keep your phone and camera close, bag zipped and in front.
  • Share your location with a friend at home on travel days.
  • Emergency numbers in Morocco: police 19 (190 from a mobile), ambulance 15 (150 from a mobile). We'll also be with you all week.

Know before you go: Morocco

Weather

Late March is one of the best times to be in Marrakech. Warm, sunny afternoons, cool evenings, the occasional shower. The desert is warm by the pool and properly cold after dark.

DayNightRain
Marrakech, late March22°C / 72°F10°C / 50°FA few days a month
Agafay desertSimilarColder than the cityRare

Money

  • Currency: Moroccan dirham (MAD). Roughly 9.3 MAD = $1 USD, subject to change.
  • Get dirhams one of two ways: order them from your bank before you leave (call first, not every branch carries them), or use an ATM at the airport when you land. ATM stops during the week won't be scheduled, so arrive with some cash.
  • Cards are fine at the riad, the camp and nicer restaurants. Souks, taxis and small shops are mostly cash.
  • Bring or withdraw enough cash for souk shopping, extra drinks and tips.
  • Use bank ATMs, and always choose to be charged in local currency.
  • Tell your bank you're traveling.

Tipping

Tipping is completely up to you and your experience. It's never required, but it's appreciated by the locals who make the week happen. Had an incredible experience? Tip more. Felt more "meh"? That's OK too.

  • Guides and activities: tip them directly at the end of the activity, if you'd like.
  • Restaurants on your own: about 10% is normal. Small tips (10 to 20 MAD) for porters and help along the way are customary.

Cash in dirhams is easiest for tips.

Phone + data

  • Check your carrier's international plan before you go.
  • An eSIM is usually cheapest. Airalo or Holafly work well. Install before you fly, turn on when you land.
  • Download offline Google Maps of the medina. Street signs are scarce.
  • Our group message is how we'll all talk. Wi-Fi is available at the riad and the camp.

Plugs + power

Type C and E plugs (European style), 220V. Bring 1 to 2 adapters. Most phone, laptop and camera battery chargers are fine. Hair tools often aren't, so bring dual-voltage or use the venue's.

Local customs

  • Dress with shoulders and knees covered in the medina. At the riad and the camp, wear what you like.
  • Non-Muslims can't enter most mosques, including the Koutoubia.
  • Haggling is normal and expected in the souks.
  • If you photograph locals, they may expect to be paid.
  • A few words go a long way: Hello = Salam | Thank you = Shukran

FAQ

The questions we get most. Still wondering something? Ask us.

Before you go

I'm coming alone. Will that be weird?
Not even a little. Most people do.

Is this only for wedding photographers?
Yes.

Can I bring a friend?
Absolutely, if they're a wedding photographer too! Tell them to apply the same way you did, and if they're in, we'll room you together.

How old are people who come?
Mostly 20s and 30s. The common thread is curiosity, not age.

What's the pace like?
Full but unhurried. One big thing a day, long dinners, real free time. Three nights of city energy, then two slow days in the desert.

Travel

What airport should I fly into?
Marrakesh Menara Airport (RAK).

Do you book flights for me?
No, but we'll help you pick them. Send us options any time.

What if my flight is delayed?
Email us right away. On travel day, text or WhatsApp Carrigan. We'll help as best we can.

Can I arrive a day late or leave early?
You can, but we don't refund missed nights. Tell us in advance so we can plan your car.

Can I bring my drone?
No. Drones are banned in Morocco and customs will confiscate them.

Is it safe?
Marrakech is busy and very used to visitors, and you'll be with the group and local drivers for every transfer and activity. Normal city sense applies: watch your bag in the souks and ignore unofficial "guides." Habitat has been running retreats in Morocco since 2026.

On retreat

Do I have to do everything?
No. Every activity is optional.

Do I need experience for the quads, the balloon or pottery?
No. Show up and we'll handle the rest.

What if the balloon can't fly?
It's weather-dependent. If it's grounded, we have a plan B.

How much free time is there?
There are two full days, then every other day has some downtime built in. Take it all at your own pace, and chill when you need to.

I have a food allergy. Will I be OK?
Yes. Put it in your Flight + Info Form. The kitchen will know your name and your allergy. Tell us or your server if anything looks off.

Is alcohol included?
Welcome drinks, rooftop drinks and sunset cocktails, yes. Anything extra is on you.

Can I get a hammam or massage?
Yes. Both venues have spas. They're optional and not included. Book through us or the front desk, charged to your room.

Will you help me before the trip?
Yes. Flight suggestions, packing, entry info, your mentorship session with Sarah and the group chat. Ask us anything.
