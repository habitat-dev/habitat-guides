You're in for the Maldives! We can't wait to catch the sunset from the jacuzzi with you.

Housekeeping

  • Every class is optional. This is your vacation. Skip a morning, sleep in, go straight to the water. Nobody's taking attendance.
  • First time doing Pilates or yoga? Tell us. Every class is all levels, with options for every body.
  • It's a boat. You're living close to a small group for six nights. That's the point, and it's also an adjustment. We mostly cruise inside the atolls where the water is calmer, and anchor at night. Prone to seasickness? Bring what works for you. A lower-deck cabin in the middle of the boat moves the least.
  • June is hot and mostly sunny, with some rain. About 30°C / 86°F by day. Showers usually come in short bursts and blow through. Some days are windier and the sea can get choppier. The captain picks sheltered spots and has the final say on the route. If he changes the plan, it's for a good reason.
  • Wi-Fi: none. Not on the boat. You'll get phone signal near inhabited islands. The rest of the time, enjoy it.
  • Dress code. The Maldives is a conservative Muslim country. On the boat, sandbanks and desert islands, wear what you want. On local islands, shoulders and knees get covered.
  • You're sharing space with people you may not know. Many people come solo, some come with friends. By day 3 it won't feel that way. If something or someone is bothering you, tell us early (see the Anonymous Feedback Form in section 10). We can fix things in real time. Don't wait until the week is over to put it out there. That's a disservice to you and to the people around you.
  • Plans can change. Weather, sea conditions and the captain's call sometimes rearrange the itinerary. We'll try to stick to it as laid out. If we need to adapt, we'll swap in something just as good and tell you right away. We ask that you stay flexible and patient with the situation.

Getting there + getting home

You book your own flights, so you can come from anywhere and add travel on either side. Booking your own doesn't mean you're on your own. Send us any flight before you book if you want our eyes on it.

The only two times you need

Arrive

Land at Velana International Airport (MLE) by Saturday, June 12, the day before. Group pickup at MLE from 11am on Sunday, June 13.

Depart

Check out from 8am on Saturday, June 19, then a group transfer to MLE.

Plan to land on Saturday, June 12 and sleep near the airport. Landing late on Sunday isn't an option: the boat leaves with the group, and long-haul flights run late. Many people also add a few days on the front or back end. Want help planning that part? Tell us. We love this stuff.

Lots of flight options. We know there are a ton of different ways to get here. If you're looking at flights that don't line up with these times, send them to us and we'll help you coordinate.

Transfers: The crew meets the group at MLE from 11am on Sunday and takes us to the boat. On Saturday, they bring us back to the airport. We'll put everyone into a small group chat the week before so you can find each other. Arriving or leaving outside these times? Tell us as early as you can and we'll help you plan around the boat.

How people usually get there

  • There are no direct flights from the US to the Maldives. Most people connect once through Doha, Dubai, Abu Dhabi or Istanbul, or through a European hub. From the US, plan on roughly a full day of travel door to door.
  • Velana International Airport (MLE) sits on its own island next to Malé. Hulhumalé, the town next door, is about 10 to 15 minutes away and is where most people stay the night before. Our charter partner recommends the Ocean Grand in Hulhumalé for an affordable, comfortable stay.

Our flight advice

  • Look at flights first. Find prices and times you like.
  • Book in the sweet spot. For US to Maldives flights in June, that's about 4 to 6 months out, so around mid-December 2026 to February 2027. June is low season there, so there's no need to rush, and booking much earlier rarely saves money. If you want to lock yours in early, do it. It's completely up to you.
  • Land by Saturday, June 12. Arriving Sunday morning is too risky with long-haul delays, and the boat can't wait.
  • Leave at least 2 to 3 hours for any connection. You'll often change terminals, and separate tickets don't protect you if the first flight is late.
  • Send us your flights before you book if you're unsure. We want to help.
  • Once booked, fill out the Flight + Info Form. It's also where you tell us your dietary needs, health notes and emergency contact. This is how we plan your pickup.

Flight info was current when we wrote this guide and can change. Always check before you book.

Running late or stuck?

Passports, health + safety

If you're a US citizen, you need a valid passport, a return ticket, and a free online form. No visa application. The visa is free and stamped on arrival.

Your passport

  • It must be valid for at least 6 months after you leave the Maldives, so valid until at least December 19, 2027. Check it today.
  • Have at least one blank page for the entry stamp.
  • Renewing? Do it this winter, not in May. Send us your new passport number when it arrives.
  • The name on your flights must match your passport exactly.

Visa + IMUGA

  • US citizens get a free 30-day tourist visa on arrival. No pre-approval.
  • At immigration you may be asked for your return ticket and proof of where you're staying. Have both saved on your phone. We'll send you a booking letter for the boat.
  • IMUGA Traveller Declaration (required): Every visitor fills out this free online form in the 96 hours before arrival at imuga.immigration.gov.mv. Save the confirmation to your phone. Your airline may ask to see it.
  • You also file one before you leave, within 96 hours of your flight home. We'll remind you mid-week when we have signal.
  • The form is always free. Any site charging a fee is not the government.

Customs: what not to pack

  • Alcohol. Bringing it in is not allowed, including duty-free bottles bought on your layover. Customs takes it at the airport.
  • Pork products, pornography, and religious idols for worship. Personal religious items are generally fine.
  • Vapes. Customs holds them and gives them back when you leave.
  • Drugs. Penalties are severe.
  • Prescription meds are fine with a copy of your doctor's prescription, in original packaging, and only what you need for the trip. Taking an ADHD stimulant, strong painkiller, or sleep or anxiety medication? Bring a doctor's letter too, and check with your doctor or Maldives Customs before you fly.

Not a US citizen?

Most passports get the same free visa on arrival. Israeli passports are not accepted for entry. If you're flying from or connecting more than 12 hours through a country with yellow fever risk, you'll need a yellow fever certificate. Check your own government's travel advice and tell us your nationality in your Flight + Info Form so we can help.

Health

  • No vaccines are required if you're flying from the US. Make sure routine vaccines are up to date, and ask your doctor about hepatitis A and typhoid. CDC: Maldives
  • Dengue exists in the Maldives. Bring bug spray for local islands.
  • Tell us about allergies, injuries, pregnancy or health conditions in your Flight + Info Form. We only share it with who needs to know (the chef, the crew, your hosts). Please be thorough. The more we know, the better we can look after you on a boat.
  • Seasickness: bring tablets or bands even if you've never needed them.
  • The nearest hospitals are in Malé (ADK Hospital, Tree Top Hospital and IGMH). The boat can be hours away from one.
  • Drink the bottled or filtered water on board. Don't drink tap water.
  • The sun is no joke at the equator. Reef-safe SPF, a hat, a long-sleeve swim top, and water.

Safety

  • The Maldives has a low crime rate, and our group is on a private boat. Normal travel sense applies.
  • In the water, follow the snorkel guide. Currents can be strong. Always snorkel with a buddy.
  • Keep your passport in your cabin. Keep a digital copy on your phone.
  • Don't leave bags unattended at the airport or on local islands.
  • Emergency numbers in the Maldives: 119 (police), 102 (ambulance). We're with you all week.

Know before you go: the Maldives

Weather in June

Hot, humid and mostly sunny, with tropical showers that usually come and go fast. June is the southwest monsoon, so expect some wind, a few rainy spells and the odd choppy day. The water is bathwater warm.

DayNightSeaRain
Maldives, mid-JuneAbout 30°C / 86°FAbout 26°C / 79°FAbout 29°C / 84°FShowers likely, usually short

Time zone

The Maldives is 10 hours ahead of Austin and 12 hours ahead of Los Angeles in June.

Money

  • Currency: Maldivian rufiyaa (MVR). Roughly 15.4 MVR = $1 USD. Check xe.com before you go.
  • US dollars are the working currency of Maldives tourism. On the boat, you won't need rufiyaa.
  • Bring some small US bills for local islands, cafés and any extra tips. ATMs are at the airport and in Malé, not out in the atolls.
  • Rufiyaa is hard to change back, so don't take out more than you need.

Tipping

  • The crew tip is already included in your retreat. You don't need to bring cash for it.
  • Crew: If the week blew you away and you want to give extra, it's completely up to you. Small US dollar bills in an envelope at the end of the week is easiest.
  • Local islands: Tipping isn't expected at cafés. Rounding up is a nice touch.

Phone + data

  • There's no Wi-Fi on the boat. Check your carrier's international plan, or get an eSIM (Airalo or Holafly). Install at home, switch on when you land.
  • Signal is good near inhabited islands and drops out on open water.
  • Our group message is how we'll all talk.

Plugs + power

The Maldives mostly uses Type G plugs (three rectangular pins, like the UK), sometimes Type D, at 230V. Bring a universal adapter. Phone and laptop chargers are fine. US hair tools usually aren't, so bring dual-voltage or skip them.

Local customs

  • The Maldives is a Muslim country. On local islands, cover shoulders and knees, keep swimwear for the boat and designated beaches, and skip the PDA.
  • Alcohol is only served on boats and resorts. None on local islands.
  • Friday is the weekend, and midday Friday prayers mean things close for a while.
  • Ask before photographing people.
  • Two words: Assalaamu alaikum (hello), Shukuriyyaa (thank you).

Travel lightly

  • Bring a reusable water bottle. Drinking water is on board.
  • Use reef-safe sunscreen.
  • Never touch or stand on coral. Fins up, feet off the reef.
  • Give mantas, turtles and whale sharks space. No touching, no chasing, no flash.
  • Shells, coral and sand stay in the Maldives. Taking them out isn't allowed.

FAQ

The questions we get most. Still wondering something? Ask us.

Before you go

I'm coming alone. Will that be weird?
Not even a little. Most people do.

I've never done Pilates or yoga. Is that OK?
Yes. Every class is all levels and optional, and you'll get options for your body. Let us know if you want some extra instruction outside of class.

Can I bring a friend?
Absolutely. This retreat is women-only, so tell her to book the same way you did and we'll room you together.

How old are people who come?
Every age is welcome. The common thread is curiosity, not age.

Can I change my cabin type?
If a cabin is available, of course. Ask and we'll make it happen.

Can I give my spot to a friend?
If you need to cancel, any balance we keep under the cancellation policy can go toward a friend who takes your spot. Your deposit can't be transferred. Email us and we'll set it up.

What's the pace like?
Slow, on purpose. Mornings are unhurried. Afternoons are open. It's a boat trip with Pilates on it, and the ocean is the main event.

Travel

How do I get to the boat?
Fly into Velana International Airport (MLE). The crew picks up the group there from 11am on Sunday, June 13.

Should I fly in the day before?
Yes. Landing late on Sunday isn't an option, so plan on arriving Saturday, June 12. Sleep in Hulhumalé, 10 to 15 minutes from the airport. Our charter partner recommends the Ocean Grand.

What if my flight is delayed?
Email us right away. On travel day, text Kennon or Carrigan. We'll help you as best we can and work out how to get you to the boat.

Do I need a visa?
Not in advance if you're a US citizen. It's free on arrival. You do need to file the free IMUGA form within 96 hours of landing.

On retreat

I get seasick. Should I worry?
We mostly cruise inside the atolls, where the water is calmer, and anchor at night. Bring whatever works for you, and pick a lower-deck cabin in the middle of the boat, where there's the least movement.

Is there Wi-Fi?
No. Get an eSIM or an international plan. You'll have signal near inhabited islands.

Do I have to be a strong swimmer?
No. There's no minimum, and life jackets are on board for anyone who wants one. Nervous in the water? Tell us and we'll keep you close.

Will we see whale sharks?
Maybe. Ari Atoll is known for mantas, and South Ari is one of the few places whale sharks stay year-round. Nothing is guaranteed. Most days deliver anyway.

Can I scuba dive?
No diving on this trip. It's all snorkeling, and lots of it.

Do I have to do everything?
No. Every class and activity is optional.

How much free time is there?
Plenty. Afternoons are mostly open. Swim, nap, sundeck, jacuzzi.

I have a food allergy. Will I be OK?
Yes. Put it in your Flight + Info Form. The chef will know your name and your allergy. Tell us or the crew if anything looks off.

Is alcohol included?
No, but it's available on board. Drinks go on your own tab. Just don't bring any into the country.

Will there be laundry?
Yes, it's available on board.

What should I wear to dinner?
Whatever you want. It's a boat. Bare feet are normal.

What if I don't click with my roommate?
Tell us, privately or through the anonymous form. We'll handle it quietly.

Money

Do I need cash?
A little. Small US dollar bills for local islands and anything extra. Everything on the boat is covered except your bar tab.

Should I tip?
Crew tips are already included. Anything extra is up to you. See Tipping in section 9.
